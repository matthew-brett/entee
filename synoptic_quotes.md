A working table of explicit Old Testament quotations in Matthew, Mark, and Luke, classified by whether the Greek wording follows the Septuagint (LXX), a hebraizing Greek form closer to the Masoretic Text (MT-G), or a mixed/revised tradition. Includes methodological conclusions on scholarly “consensus.”

---

## Conclusion: Where Is the Consensus Table?

**There is no single published table that gives, for every OT quotation in Matthew, Mark, and Luke, a widely agreed three-way label of “LXX / MT-in-Greek / mixed.”** Scholars use overlapping schemes, disagree on individual citations, and often reject the simple binary altogether.

| Your category | Usual scholarly language | Typical problem |
|---|---|---|
| Pure or nearly pure **LXX** | “Septuagintal,” “follows the LXX against the MT” | Which LXX? (Rahlfs, Göttingen, Old Greek, kaige recensions) |
| Pure or nearly pure **MT-G** | “Hebraizing,” “closer to Hebrew than LXX,” “independent translation” | Often a **revised LXX** assimilated toward Hebrew, not a fresh translation |
| **Mixed** | “Assimilated LXX,” “conflate,” “free citation,” “revised Septuagint” | Very common; boundaries disputed |

Current consensus is **case-by-case**, not a master table everyone cites.

### Best reference works

| Resource | Scope | Classification? |
|---|---|---|
| **Archer & Chirichigno**, *Old Testament Quotations in the New Testament* (1983) | Whole NT, ~312 entries; categories A–F | **Closest comprehensive table.** B ≈ LXX; C ≈ MT-G; D ≈ mixed. Evangelical framing; uses Rahlfs + BHS. |
| **Menken**, *Matthew’s Bible* (2004) | Matthew only | No summary table; argues for a **pre-Matthean revised LXX** (“Matthew’s Bible”). Supersedes simple labels for Matthew. |
| **McIver Wright**, diss. on Synoptic quotes & two-document hypothesis (1989) | Shared Synoptic quotes | Text-type + source criticism; **does not divide cleanly** into LXX vs Hebrew groups. |
| **UBS/NA Greek NT**, Index of Quotations | Lists citations | Inventory only — no LXX/MT labels |
| **Beale & Carson**, *Commentary on the NT Use of the OT* (2007) | Verse-by-verse | Discussion, not a master table |
| **Kalvesmaki**, [NT/LXX/MT parallel chart](https://kalvesmaki.com/LXX/NTChart.htm) | Side-by-side English | Explicitly **not** a scholarly classification tool |

### Recommended workflow

1. Start from the **UBS/NA Index of Quotations** (inventory).
2. Assign provisional labels from **Archer & Chirichigno** (B → LXX, C → MT-G, D → mixed).
3. Revise Matthew entries against **Menken**.
4. Check shared citations against **McIver Wright** and **Beale & Carson**.
5. Compare Greek against **Göttingen LXX** (where available) or Rahlfs, **BHS/MT**, and **NA28**.

## This Table: Scope and Method

### Included

Explicit quotations and formula citations indexed in the **UBS Greek New Testament** (4th ed.) for Matthew, Mark, and Luke — the standard scholarly inventory (~107 entries). Supplemented with a short list of **additional explicit citations** from the [Kiwi Hellenist gospel citation catalogue](https://kiwihellenist.blogspot.com/2025/05/citations.html) that fall outside the UBS index.

### Not included

Strong allusions, untagged echoes, and conflated narrative mash-ups without a quotation formula (e.g. Luke 2:22–24 drawing on 1 Sam 1, Lev 12, Exod 13 without citing them).

### Classification key

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **LXX** | Wording follows a known Septuagint tradition against the Masoretic Hebrew where the two diverge; or near-verbatim LXX (Quality ≈ P/S in Jones). |
| **MT-G** | Wording is hebraizing: closer to MT than to LXX where they diverge; or independent Greek retro-translation of Hebrew. |
| **Mixed** | Blend of LXX and Hebrew readings, revised LXX, conflation of two OT passages, or evangelist redaction on a LXX base. Includes Menken’s “revised Septuagint.” |
| **Agree** | MT, LXX, and Gospel substantially agree — text-type is **indeterminate** (Archer–Chirichigno category A). |
| **Free** | Substantial deviation from both MT and LXX (Archer E; Jones Meaning = D, Quality = E/F). |

### Source columns

| Column | Source |
|---|---|
| **A&C** | Approximate Archer & Chirichigno category (A–F) |
| **Jones** | R. Grant Jones, [Septuagint/NT comparison table](https://geocities.ws/r_grant_jones/Rick/Septuagint/sptableNT.html): Meaning (U/J = LXX sense; H = Hebrew sense; D = neither) and Quality (P = near-perfect LXX match) |
| **Menken** | Note where *Matthew’s Bible* revises the simple label (Matthew only) |
| **Status** | **Consensus** = most specialists agree on text-type; **Disputed** = significant disagreement |

### Caveats

- Comparisons use **BHS/MT** vs **Rahlfs LXX** (standard in Archer–Chirichigno); first-century texts were more pluriform (DSS, multiple Greek recensions).
- “MT-G” does **not** prove the evangelist read Hebrew; often it reflects a **Greek revision toward Hebrew**.
- Mark’s formal quotations are overwhelmingly LXX-type; Matthew’s **formula citations** (Matt 1–4, 8:17, 12:17–21, 13:35, 21:4–5, 27:9) are the hardest cases.

Interpretation:

- Where MT and LXX diverge, **LXX-type readings outnumber MT-G-type** in the Synoptics, but **mixed/revised forms are the largest single disputed category**, especially in Matthew.
- **Category A (“Agree”)** inflates any headline “NT follows LXX” statistic (the familiar Archer–Chirichigno ~340 vs ~33 tally).
- **Meaningful LXX-preference** cases are roughly Archer **B + F** (~72 NT-wide); **MT-G** cases are Archer **C** (~33 NT-wide).
